# Cryptocurrency and Digital Assets

Cryptocurrency is a form of digital currency secured by cryptography and typically operating on a decentralised blockchain network. Bitcoin and Ethereum are the most prominent examples. The asset class has attracted enormous attention but remains highly volatile and speculative.

## What is Blockchain?

A blockchain is a distributed ledger — a record of transactions maintained simultaneously across many computers worldwide. No single entity controls it. Once a transaction is recorded and confirmed, it is extremely difficult to alter. This provides transparency and security without requiring a central authority (like a bank).

## Bitcoin (BTC)

Bitcoin, created by the pseudonymous Satoshi Nakamoto in 2009, is the original and largest cryptocurrency by market capitalisation. Key characteristics:
- Fixed supply: maximum 21 million BTC will ever exist
- Proof-of-work: miners compete to validate transactions, consuming significant energy
- Primarily used as a store of value and speculative asset ("digital gold")
- No intrinsic cash flow — valued purely on supply/demand and sentiment
- High volatility: has seen multiple 80%+ drawdowns from peak

## Ethereum (ETH)

Ethereum is a programmable blockchain enabling smart contracts — self-executing code that runs when conditions are met. Ethereum is the platform on which most decentralised applications (dApps), NFTs, and DeFi protocols are built. Unlike Bitcoin, Ethereum has no fixed supply cap but uses a deflationary burning mechanism.

## Cryptocurrency in Australia

The ATO treats cryptocurrency as a CGT asset (not currency). Key implications:
- Selling, trading, or using crypto to purchase goods is a CGT event
- The 12-month 50% CGT discount applies to crypto held over a year
- Receiving crypto as payment is taxable as ordinary income
- DeFi activities (staking rewards, liquidity pool income) are generally taxable

## Risks

**Volatility**: Cryptocurrencies routinely move 20%–50% in weeks or months
**Regulatory risk**: Governments may restrict or heavily regulate crypto
**Exchange risk**: Centralised exchanges can fail, be hacked, or freeze withdrawals (FTX collapse 2022)
**Technical risk**: Smart contract bugs, protocol vulnerabilities
**Concentration**: Retail investors often hold highly concentrated positions
**No underlying cash flow**: Unlike shares (dividends) or property (rent), most crypto produces no income

## Crypto ETFs and Access for Australians

ASX-listed crypto ETFs have become available in Australia:
- EBTC (Global X Bitcoin ETF)
- ETHI includes some crypto-adjacent exposure
- Vanilla Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs launched in Australia in 2022–23

These provide regulated, tax-straightforward exposure without self-custody requirements.

## Position Sizing

Given the volatility and speculative nature, financial educators typically suggest that speculative assets like crypto should represent a small portion of a portfolio (0%–10% depending on risk tolerance) — only money you can afford to lose entirely.
