# Insurance for Australian Investors

Insurance is risk management — transferring the financial consequences of low-probability, high-impact events to an insurer. For Australians, personal insurance covers income disruption, death, disability, and serious illness.

## Life Insurance

Life insurance pays a lump sum to nominated beneficiaries upon the policyholder's death. It is designed to protect financial dependants.

**Who needs it**: Anyone with financial dependants (spouse, children, elderly parents) or significant debt (mortgage) that others would inherit responsibility for.

**How much**: A common rule of thumb is 10–15 times annual income, or enough to pay off the mortgage and provide income for dependants for 10+ years.

**Tax**: Life insurance premiums are generally not tax deductible outside super. Death benefits paid to financial dependants are tax-free.

## Total and Permanent Disability (TPD) Insurance

TPD pays a lump sum if you become permanently unable to work due to illness or injury. 

**"Any occupation" vs "Own occupation"**: Any occupation TPD only pays if you cannot do any job. Own occupation pays if you cannot do your specific occupation — more valuable for specialists like surgeons whose earning capacity would be severely diminished even if they could work in a different role.

## Income Protection Insurance

Income protection (IP) replaces a percentage of your income (typically 70%–75%) if you are unable to work due to illness or injury. Unlike lump-sum policies, it pays monthly benefits for an agreed period.

**Waiting period**: The period before benefits commence (14 days, 30 days, 60 days, 90 days, 2 years). Longer waiting periods reduce premiums.

**Benefit period**: How long payments continue (1 year, 2 years, 5 years, or to age 65). To-age-65 policies provide the best protection.

**Tax**: IP premiums paid outside super are generally tax deductible. Benefits received are taxable income.

**Particularly important for**: Self-employed individuals, high-income earners without sick leave, those with large mortgages or dependants.

## Trauma / Critical Illness Insurance

Pays a lump sum upon diagnosis of specific serious conditions including cancer, heart attack, stroke, organ failure, and others (the exact list varies by policy).

Covers the financial shock of serious illness — medical costs not covered by Medicare, rehabilitation, time off work, lifestyle modifications. Different to TPD (which requires permanent disability) — you can receive a trauma payout and make a full recovery.

## Insurance Through Super

Most Australians have life and TPD insurance bundled into their superannuation fund. This is convenient and premiums are deducted from super balance, but:
- Default amounts are often inadequate
- "Any occupation" definitions are common (less favourable)
- Income protection through super has tax implications and limited benefit periods
- New regulations require super funds to confirm insurance for low-balance and young accounts

## Reviewing Insurance

Insurance needs change throughout life. Review when:
- Taking on a large mortgage
- Having children
- Starting a business
- Income significantly increases
- Assets grow significantly (reducing insurance need)
- Relationships change (divorce, remarriage)
