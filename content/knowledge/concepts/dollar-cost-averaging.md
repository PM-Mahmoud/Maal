# Dollar-Cost Averaging

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) is the practice of investing a fixed dollar amount at regular intervals (weekly, fortnightly, monthly), regardless of asset prices. It is one of the most powerful investing habits for long-term wealth building.

## How DCA Works

Instead of investing a lump sum all at once, you split it into regular investments:

**Example**: You have $12,000 to invest. Instead of investing it all today:
- Invest $1,000 per month for 12 months
- When prices are low, your $1,000 buys more units
- When prices are high, your $1,000 buys fewer units
- The average cost per unit is mathematically lower than the average price over that period

## DCA vs Lump Sum

Research (including Vanguard studies) consistently shows that **lump sum investing outperforms DCA approximately 70% of the time** because markets tend to rise over time — so earlier investment benefits from more time in the market.

However, DCA provides:
- **Psychological comfort**: Reduces regret if the market falls after a large lump sum investment
- **Forced discipline**: Regular investing from salary is DCA — you invest when funds are available
- **Behavioural guardrails**: Prevents trying to time markets

For most people investing from regular income (salary), DCA is not a choice — it is the natural result of investing regularly from savings. The debate matters primarily when you receive a windfall (inheritance, bonus, property sale).

## Automating DCA

Many Australian brokers support automatic investment plans:
- Pearler: designed for automated long-term investing
- CommSec Pocket: regular small investments
- ETF provider distribution reinvestment plans (DRPs)
- Setting up automatic transfers to investment accounts then manually investing monthly

## DCA and Behavioural Finance

Market timing is extremely difficult — even professional fund managers rarely get it right consistently. DCA sidesteps the question entirely. You invest regardless of whether you think markets are high or low, removing the emotional decision from the equation.

Studies show that investors who try to time markets earn significantly less than those who simply invest regularly and hold. Missing just the 10 best days in the S&P 500 over 20 years (often clustered near market bottoms) dramatically reduces total return.

## DCA in Super

Employer SG contributions and salary sacrifice are automatically DCA — contributions are made with every pay cycle, regardless of market conditions. This is one reason super balances tend to grow steadily even through market volatility.
