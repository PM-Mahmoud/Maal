# Estate Planning Basics

Estate planning ensures your assets are distributed according to your wishes when you die, and that someone you trust manages your affairs if you become incapacitated. It is relevant at every wealth level and becomes increasingly important as assets grow.

## Why Estate Planning Matters

Without a valid will:
- Your estate is distributed according to your state's intestacy laws (usually spouse, then children, then parents)
- Disputes among family members may result in costly legal proceedings
- The wrong people may receive your assets
- The process takes longer and costs more

## The Will

A will is a legal document specifying how your assets should be distributed. It must be:
- In writing
- Signed by you in the presence of two independent witnesses who also sign
- Made by someone of legal age and sound mind

A will can be made simple or complex. Simple wills can be prepared for a few hundred dollars through online services or community legal centres. More complex estates benefit from a solicitor.

## Executor

The executor is the person you appoint to administer your estate — lodge probate, pay debts, and distribute assets according to the will. Choose someone trustworthy, organised, and willing to take on the responsibility.

## Superannuation Does Not Pass Via Your Will

This is one of the most misunderstood aspects of Australian estate planning. Your super balance is not part of your estate — it is held by the trustee of your super fund.

To direct where your super goes, you must make a **binding death benefit nomination (BDBN)** — a separate legal document lodged with your super fund. Without one, the trustee has discretion over who receives your super.

Beneficiaries must be financial dependants or your legal personal representative (estate). Nominations typically expire every three years (lapsing) or can be non-lapsing in some funds.

## Enduring Power of Attorney (EPOA)

An EPOA appoints someone to make financial and legal decisions on your behalf if you lose capacity. Without one, your family may need to apply to a tribunal to be appointed guardian, which is costly and time-consuming.

Separate to the EPOA is a **medical power of attorney** (or advance health directive) — appointing someone to make healthcare decisions.

## Testamentary Trusts

A testamentary trust is created by a will and can provide significant tax advantages for beneficiaries (particularly children and low-income earners) and asset protection. Income distributed from a testamentary trust to minor children is taxed at adult marginal rates rather than the punitive minor's tax rate. Suitable for larger estates.

## Keeping It Current

Review your estate plan after:
- Marriage or divorce (marriage revokes an existing will in some states)
- Birth of children
- Death of a beneficiary or executor
- Major asset acquisition or disposal
- Business ownership changes
- Moving interstate (state laws vary)
