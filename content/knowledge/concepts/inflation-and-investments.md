# Inflation and Its Impact on Investments

Inflation is the sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services. It erodes purchasing power over time — $100 today buys more than $100 will buy in 10 years if inflation persists.

## Why Inflation Matters for Investors

Every investment return must be assessed in real (inflation-adjusted) terms. A 5% return when inflation is 4% is only a 1% real return. Building wealth means earning returns above inflation over time.

**Nominal return**: The stated return (e.g., 7%)
**Real return**: Nominal return minus inflation (7% − 3% = 4% real)

## Causes of Inflation

**Demand-pull**: Economy growing too fast — too much money chasing too few goods
**Cost-push**: Rising production costs (energy, labour, raw materials) passed on to consumers
**Monetary expansion**: Too much money in the economy relative to goods and services (Milton Friedman: "Inflation is always and everywhere a monetary phenomenon")
**Supply shocks**: Sudden disruptions (COVID supply chains, oil embargoes, wars) restrict supply and push prices up

## How Different Assets Perform During Inflation

**Equities**: Mixed. Companies with pricing power (can raise prices with inflation) do well. Capital-intensive or debt-heavy companies suffer. Overall, equities have historically outpaced inflation over long periods, though high inflation often triggers rate rises that hurt valuations in the short term.

**Property**: Generally a good inflation hedge. Rents and property values tend to rise with inflation. Landlords with fixed-rate mortgages benefit as their real debt burden falls.

**Bonds (fixed rate)**: Hurt by inflation. Rising inflation leads to rising interest rates, which push bond prices down. Fixed coupons lose real value. Existing bondholders see losses.

**Inflation-linked bonds (TIPS, indexed bonds)**: Designed to protect against inflation — principal and coupons adjust with CPI. Genuine inflation protection for fixed income allocations.

**Gold and commodities**: Traditional inflation hedges. Gold holds purchasing power over very long periods, though it is volatile and pays no income. Oil and base metals often rise with inflation (demand or supply-driven).

**Cash and term deposits**: Lose real value when interest rates lag behind inflation (negative real rates). Suffer most in inflationary environments.

**Cryptocurrency**: Too short a history to assess as an inflation hedge. Has shown high correlation with risk assets, making its inflation-hedging properties unclear.

## The RBA's Inflation Response

The RBA targets 2%–3% annual inflation. When CPI consistently exceeds 3%, the RBA raises the cash rate, increasing borrowing costs to cool spending and investment. This affects variable mortgage holders, businesses, and asset valuations simultaneously.

## Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)

PPP is the theory that exchange rates should eventually adjust so that identical goods cost the same in different countries. In practice, exchange rates deviate from PPP for extended periods, but PPP provides a useful long-run anchor for currency valuation.
