# Dividend Investing

Dividend investing focuses on companies that regularly distribute a portion of their profits to shareholders. It appeals to income-focused investors seeking regular cash flow from their portfolio.

## What is a Dividend?

A dividend is a distribution of company profits to shareholders, usually paid quarterly (US companies) or semi-annually (Australian companies). The amount per share multiplied by your number of shares is your dividend income.

**Dividend yield** = Annual dividend per share ÷ Share price × 100
A share paying $0.80 per year trading at $16 has a 5% yield.

## Dividend Growth vs High Yield

**High dividend yield stocks**: Pay large current dividends relative to their price. Often include utilities, REITs, banks, and infrastructure. The ASX has many high-yield stocks due to franking credits. Risk: high yields can reflect companies with limited growth prospects or unsustainable payout ratios.

**Dividend growth stocks**: Companies that consistently increase their dividend over time. Even a modest starting yield can compound into substantial income over 20 years. US examples: companies that have raised dividends annually for 25+ years are called "Dividend Aristocrats."

## Payout Ratio

The payout ratio is dividends paid ÷ earnings per share. A payout ratio of 50% means the company returns half its earnings as dividends.

- Very high payout ratios (above 80%) may be unsustainable
- Very low payout ratios suggest room to increase dividends or retain earnings for growth
- Some sectors (REITs, regulated utilities) sustainably pay out 80–100% of earnings

## Dividend Reinvestment Plans (DRPs)

Many Australian companies offer Dividend Reinvestment Plans, allowing shareholders to automatically reinvest dividends to receive additional shares, often at a small discount to market price. DRPs accelerate compound growth without brokerage costs.

## ETFs offering dividend focus

- **VHY** (Vanguard High Yield): Australian companies with high dividend yield
- **HVST** (Betashares High Income): Enhanced income ETF with derivatives overlay
- **IHD** (iShares): S&P/ASX Dividend Opportunities
- **VGS** + **VAS**: Capturing broad market dividends (not specifically yield-focused)

## Dividend Traps

A falling share price boosting the apparent yield is a "dividend trap." If a company is cutting its dividend or is fundamentally impaired, the high yield is not sustainable. Always look at payout ratios, earnings trends, and free cash flow coverage of dividends.

## Australian vs US Dividend Culture

Australia has a strong dividend culture partly due to franking credits, making Australian shares more appealing to domestic investors. The big four banks, BHP, Woodside, Wesfarmers, and Telstra are stalwart dividend payers. By contrast, many large US technology companies pay no dividends, preferring share buybacks or reinvestment.

## Dividends and Tax

Dividends are generally taxable income. In Australia:
- Unfranked dividends: fully taxable at marginal rate
- Franked dividends: include the franking credit in income, claim the credit as a tax offset
- Foreign dividends: taxable, with foreign withholding tax claimable as a FITO
