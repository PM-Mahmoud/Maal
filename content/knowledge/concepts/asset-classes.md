# Asset Classes

An asset class is a group of investments that share similar characteristics, behave similarly in the marketplace, and are subject to the same laws and regulations. Understanding asset classes is the foundation of portfolio construction.

## Equities (Shares)

Shares represent ownership in a company. Shareholders benefit from capital growth (rising share price) and income (dividends). Equities have historically been the highest-returning asset class over the long run, but with significant short-term volatility.

- **Listed equities**: Traded on stock exchanges (ASX, NYSE, LSE, etc.)
- **Unlisted equities**: Private company shares, typically accessed through venture capital or private equity funds
- **Historical long-run return (global)**: Approximately 8%–10% per year before inflation
- **Risk**: Can fall 30%–50% in severe downturns (2008–09, 2020 COVID crash)

## Fixed Income (Bonds)

Bonds are debt instruments — you lend money to a government or company in exchange for regular interest payments (coupons) and the return of principal at maturity.

- **Government bonds**: Issued by national governments (Australian Commonwealth Government Securities, US Treasuries). Very low default risk.
- **Corporate bonds**: Issued by companies. Higher yield to compensate for credit risk.
- **Investment grade**: Bonds rated BBB– or above by major ratings agencies (S&P, Moody's, Fitch).
- **High yield ("junk bonds")**: Below investment grade. Higher yield, higher risk of default.
- Bonds typically move inversely to interest rates — when rates rise, bond prices fall.

## Property

Real estate as an investment class spans residential, commercial, industrial, and retail property. In Australia, property represents a large portion of household wealth.

- **Direct property**: Owning physical property
- **REITs/A-REITs**: Listed Real Estate Investment Trusts — liquid exposure to property
- **Property funds**: Unlisted managed funds investing in commercial property

## Cash and Cash Equivalents

Cash includes savings accounts, term deposits, and money market instruments. The safest asset class (no capital risk for deposits up to $250,000 guaranteed by the Australian Government Guarantee Scheme) but typically earns the lowest long-run return.

## Commodities

Raw materials and agricultural products including gold, oil, iron ore, copper, wheat, and coffee.

- Gold is widely held as a "safe haven" and inflation hedge
- Oil and base metals are sensitive to global economic growth
- Australian investors have indirect commodity exposure through ASX-listed mining and energy companies

## Infrastructure

Long-lived physical assets like airports, toll roads, ports, pipelines, and utilities. Typically provide stable, inflation-linked returns. Accessible through listed infrastructure ETFs and managed funds.

## Alternatives

A broad category including:
- Hedge funds
- Private equity and venture capital
- Cryptocurrency and digital assets
- Collectables (art, wine, watches)
- Catastrophe bonds and insurance-linked securities

## Asset Allocation

The mix of asset classes in a portfolio determines most of its long-run risk and return characteristics. A typical "balanced" portfolio might hold 60% growth assets (equities, property) and 40% defensive assets (bonds, cash). A "high growth" portfolio might be 80–90% equities.
