# Financial Planning Fundamentals

Financial planning is the process of defining your financial goals and building a systematic strategy to achieve them. It encompasses budgeting, saving, investing, insurance, and estate planning across your life stages.

## The Financial Planning Process

1. **Assess your current position**: Calculate your net worth (assets minus liabilities) and understand your cash flow (income minus expenses)
2. **Define your goals**: Short-term (emergency fund, holiday), medium-term (house deposit, car), long-term (retirement, financial independence, children's education)
3. **Identify the gap**: What is needed to achieve your goals? How much, by when?
4. **Develop a strategy**: Asset allocation, savings rate, debt management, insurance, tax optimisation
5. **Implement**: Open accounts, automate investments, arrange insurance
6. **Monitor and review**: Revisit annually or when circumstances change

## Net Worth

Net worth = Total assets − Total liabilities

**Assets**: Cash, super, investments, property (market value), vehicles (if significant)
**Liabilities**: Mortgage, personal loans, student debt (HECS-HELP), credit card balances, car loans

Tracking net worth over time is the clearest indicator of financial progress. Growing net worth means building genuine wealth.

## Savings Rate

Savings rate = (Income − Expenses) ÷ Income × 100

This is the most powerful variable in wealth building. A 20% savings rate builds wealth twice as fast as a 10% savings rate. Increasing income while controlling expenses is the most effective way to accelerate financial goals.

## The 50/30/20 Budget Framework

A simple framework for allocating income:
- 50%: Needs (housing, food, transport, utilities, minimum debt repayments)
- 30%: Wants (dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, travel)
- 20%: Savings and extra debt repayment

The exact percentages should be adapted to your cost of living and goals.

## Debt Management Priority

Not all debt is equal:
1. **Eliminate high-interest debt first** (credit cards at 20%+): guaranteed return equal to the interest rate
2. **Maintain manageable debt** (HECS, low-rate mortgages): may not be worth aggressive repayment if investing returns more
3. **Investment debt** (margin loans, SMSF LRBAs): productive if managed carefully

## Financial Life Stages

**20s**: Build emergency fund, start super contributions, establish good habits, limit lifestyle inflation
**30s**: Property purchase decision, career growth, family responsibilities, insurance review
**40s**: Peak earning years, accelerate investment contributions, re-evaluate super, review estate planning
**50s**: Pre-retirement planning, super consolidation, transition to retirement strategies, pay down mortgage
**60s+**: Drawdown strategy, pension phase, estate planning, aged care planning

## SMART Financial Goals

Goals should be Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. "Save more money" is not a goal. "Save $50,000 house deposit by June 2027 by saving $1,500/month" is a SMART goal.
