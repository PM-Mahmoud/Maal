# Capital Gains Tax in Australia

Capital Gains Tax (CGT) is not a separate tax in Australia — capital gains are included in your assessable income and taxed at your marginal rate, subject to important discounts and exemptions.

## What Triggers CGT

CGT applies when you dispose of a CGT asset, including:
- Shares, ETFs, and managed funds
- Investment property
- Cryptocurrency and digital assets
- Collectables worth over $500
- Most personal use assets over $10,000

Events triggering CGT include selling, gifting, or transferring an asset, as well as receiving compensation for loss or destruction of an asset.

## The 50% CGT Discount

If you hold an asset for more than 12 months before disposing of it, you receive a 50% CGT discount as an individual or trust (33% for super funds). This means only half your capital gain is added to your taxable income.

**Example:** You buy shares for $10,000 and sell for $25,000 after 18 months. Capital gain = $15,000. With the 50% discount, only $7,500 is added to taxable income.

## Capital Losses

Capital losses can only be used to offset capital gains — they cannot be offset against ordinary income. Losses carry forward indefinitely until you have gains to offset them against. Apply losses to discounted gains last (to maximise the tax benefit).

## Main Residence Exemption

Your principal place of residence is generally fully exempt from CGT. If you rented the property for any period, a partial exemption may apply based on the proportion of time it was your principal residence.

The six-year rule allows you to treat a former home as your main residence for up to six years while renting it out, provided you are not treating another property as your main residence simultaneously.

## Investment Property and CGT

When selling an investment property, you must calculate CGT on the gain from the date of purchase to the date of sale. The cost base includes the purchase price plus associated costs (stamp duty, legal fees, capital improvements). You can deduct the cost of capital works (depreciation-equivalent for buildings) from the cost base.

## Super Fund CGT Rate

Super funds in accumulation phase pay CGT at an effective rate of 10% for assets held more than 12 months (15% tax × 33% discount). This makes super a particularly tax-effective environment for long-term investment.

## Cryptocurrency and CGT

The ATO treats cryptocurrency as a CGT asset, not as currency. Every disposal (sale, trade, exchange, or using crypto to buy goods) is a CGT event. Keeping accurate records is essential. The 12-month discount applies to crypto held more than a year.

## Record Keeping

The ATO requires CGT records to be kept for five years after the relevant tax return is lodged. For shares, your broker's transaction history often provides sufficient records. For property, retain all purchase and sale documents, plus evidence of capital improvements.

## Strategies

- **Tax loss harvesting**: Selling underperforming assets to realise losses that offset gains.
- **Timing disposals**: Selling just after the 12-month mark to access the 50% discount.
- **Holding assets in super**: Super's 10% effective CGT rate vs 22.5% (top marginal rate with 50% discount).
- **Spousal transfers**: Assets can sometimes be transferred between spouses at cost base in certain circumstances.
