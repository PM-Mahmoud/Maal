# Self-Managed Super Funds (SMSFs)

An SMSF is a private superannuation fund you manage yourself. Unlike industry or retail funds, you (and up to five other members) act as both the member and trustee, giving full control over investment strategy and asset selection. SMSFs are regulated by the ATO rather than APRA.

## Who Should Consider an SMSF

SMSFs are generally worth the additional complexity and cost for:
- Those with super balances above $200,000–$500,000 (the typical break-even point for costs)
- Investors who want to hold specific assets (direct property, unlisted shares, physical gold, art)
- Those with the time and financial literacy to manage their own fund
- Business owners who want their SMSF to own the business's commercial premises (a popular strategy)

## What SMSFs Can Invest In

SMSFs have broad investment powers but must comply with investment rules:
- **Allowed**: ASX shares, ETFs, managed funds, bonds, term deposits, listed investment companies, direct residential and commercial property, gold bullion, unlisted shares, collectables (under strict conditions)
- **Not allowed**: Assets acquired from related parties (with narrow exceptions), assets used personally by members, in-house assets exceeding 5% of fund value

## Trustee Responsibilities

As a trustee you must:
- Maintain the fund solely for the purpose of providing retirement benefits (sole purpose test)
- Prepare and implement a written investment strategy
- Lodge annual returns with the ATO and engage an independent SMSF auditor
- Keep detailed financial records for five years
- Meet minimum pension payment requirements if any member is in pension phase

## SMSF and Direct Property

One of the most common SMSF strategies is purchasing investment property. The property must not be lived in or rented by fund members or their related parties (except commercial property under a limited recourse borrowing arrangement). Rental income and capital gains within the fund benefit from super's low tax rates.

## Limited Recourse Borrowing Arrangements (LRBAs)

SMSFs can borrow to purchase assets via LRBAs. The asset is held in a holding trust until the loan is repaid. This allows SMSFs to leverage property or shares. Strict rules apply — the loan must be non-recourse (the lender can only claim the asset, not other fund assets).

## Costs

SMSF running costs typically include:
- ATO supervisory levy (~$259/year)
- Annual independent audit ($500–$1,500)
- Accounting and tax return ($1,500–$5,000+)
- Investment fees and brokerage
- SMSF administration platform ($500–$3,000/year if used)

At balances below $200,000, these fixed costs can eat significantly into returns compared to industry fund percentage fees.

## Pension Phase in an SMSF

When members retire and move to pension phase, the fund's earnings become tax-free (0% on investment income and capital gains). This is one of the most powerful features of the Australian super system.
