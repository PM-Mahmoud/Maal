# Medicare Levy and Medicare Levy Surcharge

Medicare is Australia's universal healthcare system, funded partly through the Medicare Levy — a 2% tax on most taxpayers' taxable income. A separate Medicare Levy Surcharge (MLS) applies to higher-income earners without private hospital cover.

## Medicare Levy

The Medicare Levy is 2% of your taxable income. It is separate from income tax, though collected through the same system.

**Reductions and exemptions:**
- Low-income earners receive a full exemption (taxable income below $26,000 for 2025-26) or a reduced levy
- Individuals with certain medical conditions may be exempt
- Foreign residents are generally exempt

## Medicare Levy Surcharge (MLS)

The MLS is an additional tax of 1%–1.5% on top of the standard 2% Medicare Levy for higher-income earners who do not have private hospital cover.

| Income (Singles) | MLS Rate |
|---|---|
| Below $93,000 | 0% |
| $93,001 – $108,000 | 1.0% |
| $108,001 – $144,000 | 1.25% |
| Over $144,000 | 1.5% |

For families, the threshold is $186,000 (2025-26), increased by $1,500 for each additional dependent child after the first.

## Private Hospital Cover Decision

For those earning over $93,000, taking out private hospital cover avoids the MLS and often costs less than the surcharge. The break-even point depends on the cost of your chosen policy.

A basic hospital cover policy costs approximately $1,000–$1,500 per year for a single adult. For someone earning $95,000, the MLS is $950 — comparable to a basic policy. Higher earners save more by having private cover.

Note: Extras/ancillary cover alone does not avoid the MLS — you need a complying hospital cover policy.

## Lifetime Health Cover Loading

Australians who do not take out private hospital cover before their 31st birthday pay a Lifetime Health Cover (LHC) loading of 2% per year of age over 30 on their hospital premium. A 40-year-old taking out cover for the first time pays 20% more than the base premium. The loading is removed after 10 continuous years of hospital cover.

## Why this matters

If your income is above the MLS thresholds, taking out private hospital cover can be cheaper than paying the surcharge. Understanding the tax implications and the value of private health insurance is an important part of financial planning for any Australian.
