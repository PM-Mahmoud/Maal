# Halal Investing in Australia

Halal investing refers to investment approaches that comply with Islamic financial principles (Sharia). It is relevant for Muslim investors who want their money to grow without violating religious obligations, as well as non-Muslim ethical investors who share similar values around avoiding exploitative practices.

## Core Principles

**Prohibition of riba (interest)**: Charging or paying interest is not permitted. This affects conventional bonds, term deposits, mortgages, and any interest-bearing securities.

**Avoidance of haram industries**: Companies with significant revenue from alcohol, pork, gambling, pornography, conventional financial services (banks charging interest), weapons, and tobacco are excluded.

**Prohibition of gharar (excessive uncertainty)**: Speculative derivatives and contracts with excessive uncertainty may be impermissible.

**Profit and loss sharing**: Investments should be based on real economic activity where risk is shared. Equity investments (shares in legitimate businesses) are generally permissible.

**Zakat**: Obligatory charitable contribution (2.5% of net savings/investments above the nisab threshold). Maal includes tools to help track this.

## Halal Screening

Halal equity investments typically go through two levels of screening:

1. **Sectoral screen**: Exclude companies in prohibited industries (alcohol, gambling, conventional finance, etc.)
2. **Financial ratio screen**: Ensure the company's debt, interest income, and liquid assets do not exceed certain thresholds (common ratios: debt/total assets < 33%, interest income/revenue < 5%)

## Halal Investment Options in Australia

**ETFs:**
- **HLAL** (Wealthsimple): Global halal equities ETF listed on the ASX. Screened by Ideally.
- **SPUS** (SPDR, US-listed): S&P 500 ESG select — partially overlapping with halal screens.
- International Sharia-compliant ETFs accessible via some brokers (ISWD on London Stock Exchange).

**Individual shares**: Certain ASX-listed companies are Sharia-compliant based on business activity and financial ratios. Technology, healthcare, and consumer goods companies often pass screening.

**Islamic home finance**: Products like Hejaz, MCCA, and Islamic Finance Australia offer Murabaha and Ijarah structures as alternatives to conventional mortgages.

**Super funds**: Crescent Wealth offers a Sharia-compliant super fund in Australia. Some mainstream funds allow you to choose a Sharia-compliant investment option.

## ESG and Halal Overlap

There is significant overlap between halal screens and ESG (environmental, social, governance) screens. Both exclude alcohol, gambling, weapons, and many unethical financial practices. However, they diverge on some sectors — halal requires financial ratio screening that ESG does not, while ESG focuses heavily on environmental and labour issues that halal does not mandate.

## Practical Considerations

- Halal equity investing is increasingly mainstream and well-served by ETFs
- Bonds and fixed income are problematic — consider sukuk (Islamic bonds) or equity-heavy portfolios
- Conventional banking products (term deposits, savings accounts) generate interest — Islamic finance alternatives offer profit-sharing arrangements
- Superannuation: most funds are not Sharia-compliant; Crescent Wealth is the primary dedicated option
