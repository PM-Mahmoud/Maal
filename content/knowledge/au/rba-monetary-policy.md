# The Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) and Monetary Policy

The Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) is Australia's central bank. Its primary mandate is to maintain price stability (target inflation of 2%–3%), support full employment, and promote the economic prosperity of the Australian people.

## The Cash Rate

The cash rate is the interest rate on overnight loans between banks in the overnight money market. The RBA sets a target cash rate and uses open market operations to maintain it. Changes to the cash rate flow through to home loan rates, business lending, and savings account rates, affecting spending and investment across the economy.

The RBA Board meets eleven times per year to review the cash rate target. Decisions are announced on the afternoon of the meeting and explained in a detailed Statement on Monetary Policy published quarterly.

## How the Cash Rate Affects You

When the RBA raises the cash rate:
- Variable mortgage rates rise → higher repayments for homeowners
- Business borrowing costs increase → slower investment and hiring
- Savings rates on deposit accounts typically rise
- Consumer spending tends to fall
- Inflation is expected to moderate over time

When the RBA cuts the cash rate:
- Variable mortgage rates fall → lower repayments
- Borrowing becomes cheaper → more investment and consumption
- AUD may depreciate (lower interest rates make Australian assets less attractive to foreign investors)
- Inflation may rise over time

## Inflation Targeting

Australia adopted a formal inflation target of 2%–3% in 1993. The RBA uses interest rates as its primary tool to keep inflation within this band. When inflation is above 3%, the RBA typically raises rates. When it is below 2%, the RBA may cut rates to stimulate the economy.

## Quantitative Easing (QE)

During extraordinary circumstances (such as the COVID-19 pandemic), the RBA has used unconventional monetary policy tools including:
- Government bond purchases to lower longer-term interest rates
- Term Funding Facility: cheap loans to banks to support credit flow
- Yield Curve Control: targeting a specific yield on short-dated government bonds

## The RBA and Australian Dollar

The RBA does not target the exchange rate but monitors it as it affects inflation (a weaker AUD makes imports more expensive). Australia operates a floating exchange rate — the AUD's value is determined by supply and demand in currency markets. RBA rate decisions significantly influence the AUD's value.

## APRA and Financial Stability

The Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) oversees banks, insurance companies, and super funds, separate from the RBA. APRA uses macroprudential tools (like limiting interest-only lending) to contain financial stability risks.
