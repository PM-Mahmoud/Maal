# Superannuation Contributions

Understanding contribution types and limits is essential for optimising your super. Putting extra money into super can significantly accelerate retirement savings thanks to the tax advantages of the super environment.

## Concessional (Pre-Tax) Contributions

Concessional contributions are made from pre-tax income and taxed at 15% within the fund — much lower than most people's marginal tax rate.

**Types:**
- Employer SG contributions (mandatory 12%)
- Salary sacrifice contributions (you arrange with your employer to divert pre-tax salary into super)
- Personal deductible contributions (self-employed or employees who claim a deduction via a Notice of Intent to Claim)

**Cap:** $30,000 per year (2025-26). Unused cap amounts can be carried forward for up to five years if your total super balance is below $500,000.

## Non-Concessional (After-Tax) Contributions

These are personal contributions made from after-tax money — you don't claim a tax deduction on them. No tax is paid on these when they enter the fund.

**Cap:** $120,000 per year (2025-26). The bring-forward rule allows contributing up to $360,000 in a single year by bringing forward three years' worth of contributions (subject to your total super balance being below $1.9 million).

## Government Co-Contribution

If you earn below $60,400 (2025-26) and make a non-concessional contribution, the government may co-contribute up to $500. The maximum co-contribution applies when you earn below $45,400 and contribute at least $1,000.

## Spouse Contributions

You can contribute to your spouse's super if they earn below $40,000. A tax offset of up to $540 is available when contributing at least $3,000 and your spouse earns below $37,000.

## First Home Super Saver Scheme (FHSS)

Voluntary contributions (concessional or non-concessional) made into super can be withdrawn to help purchase a first home. Up to $15,000 per year and $50,000 total can be released under the FHSS. Withdrawn amounts are taxed at your marginal rate minus a 30% offset.

## Downsizer Contributions

Australians aged 55 or over who sell their principal residence can contribute up to $300,000 each ($600,000 per couple) into super from the sale proceeds, outside the normal caps. This applies once per lifetime.

## Division 293 Tax

High-income earners (income + concessional contributions > $250,000) pay an additional 15% tax on concessional contributions, bringing the effective rate to 30%. Still lower than the 47% top marginal rate.

## Strategies for variable or rising incomes

Many Australians have income that varies year to year (employed, contractor, casual, or moving between roles). Key strategies include:
- Maximising salary sacrifice during high-income years
- Using carry-forward concessional contributions in lower-income years (e.g., study or part-time years)
- Considering spouse contribution splitting to equalise balances
