# How to Invest on the Australian Securities Exchange (ASX)

The Australian Securities Exchange (ASX) is Australia's primary stock exchange, listing over 2,200 companies and investment products. It is one of the top 15 largest exchanges globally by market capitalisation.

## Getting Started

To invest on the ASX, you need a brokerage account. There are two main types:

**Full-service brokers**: Provide personalised advice, research, and portfolio management. Higher fees, typically 0.5%–1% or more per trade. Suitable for investors who want guidance.

**Online discount brokers**: Self-directed platforms for buying and selling shares at low cost. Popular options in Australia include CommSec, Stake, SelfWealth, Pearler, and CMC Markets. Fees range from $0–$20 per trade.

## CHESS Sponsorship

Australia uses the Clearing House Electronic Subregister System (CHESS) to settle share trades. When you buy shares through a CHESS-sponsored broker, the shares are registered in your name and you receive a Holder Identification Number (HIN). The shares are truly yours.

Some newer brokers (particularly low-cost apps) use a custodian model, where shares are held in the broker's name on your behalf. Both are legal, but CHESS sponsorship provides stronger protection if the broker fails.

## Placing a Trade

When buying or selling shares, you can place:
- **Market order**: Executes immediately at the best available price
- **Limit order**: Executes only at your specified price or better
- **Stop-loss order**: Automatically sells when the price falls to a set level

The ASX operates Monday to Friday from 10:00am to 4:00pm AEST (10:00am pre-open from 7:00am). Settlement is T+2 — trades settle two business days after execution.

## Costs

- **Brokerage**: Charged per trade by your broker
- **Bid/ask spread**: The difference between the buy and sell price — a hidden cost in all markets
- **CGT**: Payable when you sell for a gain
- **Dividend income**: Taxable (with franking credits for Australian shares)

## ETFs on the ASX

Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) are listed on the ASX like shares but hold a diversified basket of assets. They are a popular low-cost way to gain diversified exposure. Popular ASX-listed ETFs include:
- **A200** (Betashares): Top 200 Australian companies
- **VGS** (Vanguard): Global shares excluding Australia
- **IVV** (iShares): S&P 500 (US large caps)
- **NDQ** (Betashares): NASDAQ 100 (US tech)
- **VDHG** (Vanguard): Diversified high growth (multi-asset)
- **DHHF** (Betashares): Diversified all growth (multi-asset)

## Managed Funds via mFund

The ASX's mFund service allows investors to apply for and redeem unlisted managed funds through their broker, using the same CHESS infrastructure as shares. This gives access to professional fund management with the convenience of an ASX transaction.
