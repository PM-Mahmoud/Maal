# First Home Buyer Schemes in Australia

The Australian and state governments offer several schemes to help first home buyers enter the property market. Understanding what's available can save tens of thousands of dollars.

## Federal Schemes

**First Home Guarantee (FHBG)**: Allows eligible first home buyers to purchase a home with as little as a 5% deposit without paying Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI). The government guarantees up to 15% of the loan. Income caps apply ($125,000 for singles, $200,000 for couples). Property price caps vary by location.

**Regional First Home Buyer Guarantee**: Similar to FHBG but for buyers in regional Australia. Higher property price caps apply.

**Family Home Guarantee**: For eligible single parents or single legal guardians with at least one dependent child. Allows purchase with a 2% deposit without LMI.

**First Home Super Saver Scheme (FHSS)**: Allows voluntary super contributions to be withdrawn to fund a first home purchase. Up to $15,000 per year and $50,000 total. Withdrawn amounts taxed at marginal rate minus 30% offset, generally making super an effective savings vehicle for the deposit.

## State and Territory Schemes

Each state and territory offers its own first home buyer assistance, typically including:

**Stamp duty concessions or exemptions**: First home buyers below certain purchase price thresholds receive discounts or full exemptions on stamp duty. For example, in NSW, buyers of new homes up to $800,000 pay no stamp duty; existing properties up to $1,000,000 receive a concession.

**First Home Owner Grant (FHOG)**: A one-time cash grant for purchases of new homes. Amounts vary by state ($10,000–$30,000 typically). Generally only available for new builds, not established homes.

## Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI)

LMI protects the lender (not the buyer) if a borrower defaults. It is required for loans with less than a 20% deposit. Costs vary but can be $10,000–$35,000 for a typical first home purchase. It is usually capitalised into the loan rather than paid upfront.

## Practical Considerations

- Government schemes have limited places — applications must be made through participating lenders
- Eligibility depends on income, property price, being a genuine first home buyer (no previous property ownership in Australia), and citizenship/residency status
- These schemes help with the deposit hurdle but do not reduce the ongoing mortgage repayments
- Careful budgeting is essential — buying with a minimal deposit means higher LVR and more interest over the life of the loan
