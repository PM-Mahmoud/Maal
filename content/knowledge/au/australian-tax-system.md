# The Australian Tax System

Australia has a progressive income tax system, meaning higher earners pay a higher percentage of their income in tax. The Australian Taxation Office (ATO) administers tax collection. The financial year runs from 1 July to 30 June.

## Income Tax Brackets (2025-26, Residents)

| Taxable Income | Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| $0 – $18,200 | 0% (tax-free threshold) |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | 19% |
| $45,001 – $120,000 | 32.5% |
| $120,001 – $180,000 | 37% |
| Over $180,000 | 45% |

In addition, a 2% Medicare Levy applies to most taxpayers (rebates available for low incomes). High earners without private hospital cover pay the Medicare Levy Surcharge (1%–1.5% on income above $93,000 for singles).

## Low Income Tax Offset (LITO)

The LITO provides a tax offset of up to $700 for those earning up to $37,500, tapering out at $66,667. This effectively raises the tax-free threshold for low earners.

## The Tax-Free Threshold

Australian residents do not pay tax on the first $18,200 of income. This is the tax-free threshold. Combined with the LITO, the effective tax-free threshold is closer to $23,226.

## Deductions

Common tax deductions reduce your taxable income:
- Work-related expenses (equipment, uniforms, professional memberships)
- Home office expenses (fixed rate or actual cost method)
- Vehicle expenses (logbook or cents-per-kilometre method)
- Investment property expenses (interest, rates, repairs)
- Self-education expenses related to current employment
- Charitable donations to deductible gift recipients (DGRs)

## Tax Lodgement

Most Australians lodge a tax return by 31 October following the end of the financial year (30 June). If you use a registered tax agent, you get an extended deadline. Pre-filling via myGov automatically populates income from employers, banks, and government agencies.

## PAYG Withholding

Employers withhold tax from wages under Pay As You Go (PAYG) withholding and remit it to the ATO. Your tax return reconciles what was withheld against your actual tax liability — generating either a refund or a bill.

## Capital Gains Tax (CGT)

Capital gains from selling assets are included in taxable income. Assets held for more than 12 months receive a 50% CGT discount — meaning only half the gain is taxable. Super funds pay a reduced CGT rate of 10% on assets held more than 12 months. Main residence (primary home) is generally exempt from CGT.

## Fringe Benefits Tax (FBT)

Employers who provide non-cash benefits (cars, health insurance, entertainment) may pay Fringe Benefits Tax at 47% on the grossed-up value. This affects salary packaging arrangements.

## GST

Australia has a 10% Goods and Services Tax (GST) on most goods and services. Businesses with turnover above $75,000 must register for and remit GST.
