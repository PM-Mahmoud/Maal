# Superannuation Overview

Superannuation (super) is Australia's compulsory retirement savings system. Employers must contribute a percentage of an employee's ordinary time earnings (OTE) directly into a super fund. As of 1 July 2025, the Superannuation Guarantee (SG) rate is 12%, having gradually increased from 9.5% over several years.

## How Super Works

Money goes into your super fund throughout your working life and is invested on your behalf. The fund grows through investment returns and compound interest. You generally cannot access your super until you reach preservation age (currently 60 for those born after 30 June 1964) and meet a condition of release such as retirement.

## Types of Super Funds

- **Industry funds**: Run by unions and employer associations, profits returned to members. Examples include AustralianSuper, Hostplus, and REST.
- **Retail funds**: Run by financial institutions for profit. Examples include BT Super and AMP.
- **Self-managed super funds (SMSFs)**: DIY funds run by up to six members. Offer control but require significant administration.
- **Corporate funds**: Run by large employers for their employees.
- **Public sector funds**: For government employees (e.g., UniSuper, State Super).

## Investment Options

Most funds offer a range of investment options from conservative (mostly bonds and cash) to high growth (mostly shares). The default "MySuper" option is typically a balanced or lifecycle fund. Members can usually choose their own allocation across asset classes.

## Super and Tax

Super is tax-advantaged:
- Employer SG contributions are taxed at 15% within the fund (versus your marginal rate outside super).
- Investment earnings within the fund are taxed at a maximum of 15%.
- Withdrawals after age 60 from a taxed fund are generally tax-free.
- This makes super one of the most tax-effective long-term savings vehicles available to Australians.

## Checking and Managing Your Super

Australians can check their super balance via myGov, linked to the ATO. The ATO also tracks lost and unclaimed super — billions of dollars sit in accounts people have forgotten about. Consolidating multiple super accounts is generally recommended to avoid paying multiple sets of fees.

## Super and Employment

When you start a new job, your employer will ask for your super fund details. Since November 2021, employees who do not choose a fund have their super directed to their "stapled" fund from their previous employer, rather than the employer's default fund. This reduces account duplication.

## Key Figures (2025-26)

- SG rate: 12%
- Concessional (pre-tax) contributions cap: $30,000 per year
- Non-concessional (after-tax) contributions cap: $120,000 per year
- Transfer balance cap (moving to pension phase): $1.9 million
- Low income super tax offset (LISTO): up to $500 for those earning under $37,000
