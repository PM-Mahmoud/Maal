## Income Tax Brackets — FY2025-26 (Resident Individuals)

The Stage 3 tax cuts took effect from 1 July 2024. These rates apply for FY2024-25 and FY2025-26.

| Taxable Income | Tax Rate | Tax Payable |
|---|---|---|
| $0 – $18,200 | 0% (tax-free threshold) | $0 |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | 19% | 19c per $1 over $18,200 |
| $45,001 – $135,000 | 32.5% | $5,092 + 32.5c per $1 over $45,000 |
| $135,001 – $190,000 | 37% | $34,342 + 37c per $1 over $135,000 |
| $190,001+ | 45% | $54,942 + 45c per $1 over $190,000 |

**Medicare Levy**: +2.0% on top of income tax (most taxpayers). Low-income earners may pay a reduced rate or nil.

**Effective tax rate at key income levels:**
- $80,000: ~24.4% (incl. Medicare)
- $120,000: ~28.7% (incl. Medicare)
- $180,000: ~33.9% (incl. Medicare)
- $250,000: ~40.2% (incl. Medicare)

## Medicare Levy

- **Standard rate**: 2.0% of taxable income
- **Reduction threshold**: $26,000 (individuals) — below this, a reduced levy applies
- **Exemption below**: $24,276 (individuals, FY2025-26 — indexed annually)
- **Family threshold**: $41,265 (couple, plus $3,802 per dependent child)
- **SAPTO recipients** (Seniors): higher thresholds apply

## Medicare Levy Surcharge (MLS)

Applies if you don't have private hospital cover AND income exceeds threshold:

| Income | MLS Rate |
|---|---|
| $93,000 and below | Nil |
| $93,001 – $108,000 | 1.0% |
| $108,001 – $144,000 | 1.25% |
| $144,001+ | 1.5% |

Income includes taxable income + reportable fringe benefits + total net investment losses.

## HECS-HELP Repayment Thresholds FY2025-26

Compulsory repayment rate based on Repayment Income (RI = taxable income + reportable fringe benefits + reportable employer super contributions + total net investment losses):

| Repayment Income | Repayment Rate |
|---|---|
| Below $54,435 | Nil |
| $54,435 – $62,844 | 1.0% |
| $62,845 – $66,306 | 2.0% |
| $66,307 – $72,153 | 2.5% |
| $72,154 – $80,000 | 3.0% |
| $80,001 – $86,739 | 3.5% |
| $86,740 – $91,299 | 4.0% |
| $91,300 – $97,006 | 4.5% |
| $97,007 – $107,213 | 5.0% |
| $107,214 – $112,785 | 5.5% |
| $112,786 – $119,023 | 6.0% |
| $119,024 – $124,999 | 6.5% |
| $125,000 – $136,047 | 7.0% |
| $136,048 – $143,008 | 7.5% |
| $143,009 – $150,000 | 8.0% |
| $150,001 – $157,539 | 8.5% |
| $157,540 – $166,584 | 9.0% |
| $166,585 – $176,835 | 9.5% |
| $176,836+ | 10.0% |

**Worked example**: Someone earning $180,000 is repaying at 10% = $18,000/year towards HECS, on top of regular income tax. This is on gross repayment income, not net.

**HECS marginal tax effect**: The ATO uses a "low income tax offset" reduction mechanism. Effectively, for each extra dollar earned between $67,000–$125,000, you pay 15c extra in HECS repayments (in addition to the marginal income tax rate of 32.5%). This creates an effective marginal rate of ~47.5% in this band for HECS holders.

## Superannuation Contribution Caps FY2025-26

| Type | Cap |
|---|---|
| Concessional (pre-tax, incl. SG + salary sacrifice + personal deductible) | **$30,000/year** |
| Non-concessional (after-tax) | **$120,000/year** |
| Non-concessional bring-forward (3 years, if under 75 at start of year) | **$360,000** |
| Total Superannuation Balance (TSB) cap for non-concessional | $1.9 million |
| Transfer Balance Cap (TBC — max in pension/retirement phase) | **$1.9 million** |

**Concessional cap indexation history:**
- FY2021-22 to FY2023-24: $27,500
- FY2024-25 and FY2025-26: $30,000

**Carry-forward concessional contributions**: If your TSB < $500,000, you can carry forward unused concessional cap amounts from up to 5 prior financial years and make a larger concessional contribution in one year.

## Capital Gains Tax (CGT)

- **Short-term** (asset held < 12 months): Full capital gain included in taxable income, taxed at marginal rate
- **Long-term** (asset held ≥ 12 months): 50% CGT discount — only half the gain is included in taxable income

**CGT example**: An investor on $200,000 income sells shares for $100,000 gain after 2 years.
- Net gain after 50% discount: $50,000
- Total taxable income: $250,000
- CGT on that $50,000 at 45% marginal = $22,500 (+ 2% Medicare = $23,500 effective)

**CGT exemptions**: Main residence (primary home), motor vehicles, personal use assets under $10,000, gambling winnings

**Tax-loss harvesting**: Capital losses can be used to offset capital gains. Net capital losses carry forward indefinitely but cannot offset income.

## Low Income Tax Offset (LITO)

- Maximum offset: **$700** (for incomes up to $37,500)
- Phases out: $37,501–$45,000 (reduces by 5c per $1)
- Further phase-out: $45,001–$66,667 (reduces by 1.5c per $1)
- No LITO above $66,667

## Low and Middle Income Tax Offset (LMITO)

LMITO was removed from 1 July 2022 — no longer applies.

## Superannuation Guarantee (SG) Rate

**12.0%** of ordinary time earnings from 1 July 2025 (final scheduled rate, no further increases planned).

## Preservation Age (Super Access)

| Date of Birth | Preservation Age |
|---|---|
| Before 1 July 1960 | 55 |
| 1 July 1960 – 30 June 1961 | 56 |
| 1 July 1961 – 30 June 1962 | 57 |
| 1 July 1962 – 30 June 1963 | 58 |
| 1 July 1963 – 30 June 1964 | 59 |
| 1 July 1964 or later | 60 |

From preservation age: can access super tax-free once retired (or via transition to retirement income stream).

## Age Pension

- **Pension age**: 67 (both men and women, since 1 July 2023)
- **Single full pension**: $1,116.30/fortnight (as at March 2025, indexed twice yearly)
- **Couple full pension**: $841.40/fortnight each ($1,682.80 combined)
- **Assets test — full pension** (homeowner): Single $314,000 / Couple $470,000
- **Income test — full pension**: Single $212/fortnight / Couple $372/fortnight combined

## ASFA Comfortable Retirement Standard (2025)

- **Single**: $595,000 superannuation balance at retirement
- **Couple**: $690,000 superannuation balance at retirement
- Assumes homeownership, takes account income + part Age Pension

## Franking Credits

- Corporate tax rate for large companies: **30%**
- Corporate tax rate for base-rate entities (turnover < $50M, mostly passive income < 80%): **25%**
- Imputation credit example: $70 dividend from large company = $30 franking credit = $100 gross dividend
- Full imputation credit refunded to low-income earners (tax payable < credits received)

## Private Health Insurance Rebate (FY2025-26)

The government rebate reduces private health insurance premiums. Based on age and income:

| Income (singles) | Under 65 | 65–69 | 70+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $93,000 or less | 24.608% | 28.710% | 32.812% |
| $93,001–$108,000 | 16.405% | 20.507% | 24.608% |
| $108,001–$144,000 | 8.202% | 12.303% | 16.405% |

## Negative Gearing & Investment Property

- **Negative gearing**: Rental income < deductible expenses → net loss reduces taxable income at marginal rate
- **Depreciation**: Building depreciation (2.5%/year if constructed after Sept 1987) + depreciation on plant and equipment
- **Land tax**: State-based, thresholds vary (principal residence usually exempt; investment properties liable)
- **Stamp duty**: State-based on purchase price, typically 3–5.5% for most states

## First Home Buyer Schemes

- **First Home Super Saver (FHSS)**: Contribute to super, withdraw up to $50,000 ($15,000/year max per year, across 5 years max) for first home deposit. Taxed at marginal rate - 30%.
- **First Home Guarantee**: 5% deposit, government guarantees remaining LVR, no LMI (limited places)
- **Help to Buy**: Shared equity scheme (federal), government co-buys up to 40% new / 30% existing (pilot, limited places)
- **State-specific**: First home buyer grants and stamp duty concessions vary by state

## Key Superannuation Tax Rates

| Contribution type | Tax rate |
|---|---|
| Concessional contributions (employer SG + salary sacrifice) | 15% contributions tax |
| High-income earner (income + concessional contributions > $250,000) | 30% contributions tax (Division 293) |
| Non-concessional contributions | Already after-tax — no further tax |
| Investment earnings inside super (accumulation phase) | 15% (with 10% CGT discount if >12 months) |
| Investment earnings in pension phase (below TBC) | 0% — tax free |
| Lump sum withdrawal at or above preservation age | Generally 0% (tax-free component + taxed element threshold) |

## Division 293 — High Income Super Tax

If your income + concessional contributions > $250,000, you pay an additional 15% tax on the concessional contributions that take you over (total 30%). Affects higher-income earners. ATO sends a Division 293 assessment after tax return processing.
