# Negative Gearing

Negative gearing occurs when the costs of owning an investment property (or other asset) exceed the income it generates, resulting in a net loss. In Australia, this loss can generally be deducted against other income, including salary, reducing your overall tax liability.

## How It Works

If your rental property earns $25,000 per year in rent but costs $35,000 to hold (mortgage interest, rates, insurance, property management, maintenance), you have a $10,000 net rental loss. This loss is deducted from your taxable income, reducing your tax bill.

At a 37% marginal rate, a $10,000 loss saves $3,700 in tax — meaning the actual out-of-pocket cost is $6,300, not $10,000.

## The Tax Benefit

The higher your marginal tax rate, the greater the benefit of negative gearing. This is why negative gearing tends to favour high-income earners. The strategy is premised on the expectation that capital growth over time will exceed the accumulated losses.

## Deductible Expenses

Common deductible expenses for investment properties include:
- Loan interest (the largest component for most investors)
- Property management fees (typically 7–10% of rent)
- Council rates, water rates, strata levies
- Insurance (landlord's insurance, building insurance)
- Repairs and maintenance (not capital improvements)
- Depreciation on fixtures, fittings, and building structure (if eligible)
- Accounting fees for property-related tax work
- Advertising for tenants

## Capital Works Deductions

Depreciation on the building structure (capital works) is deductible at 2.5% per year for 40 years on properties constructed after 16 September 1987. A quantity surveyor's report can identify all depreciable items. This is a non-cash deduction that reduces the property's cost base for CGT purposes.

## Positive Gearing

Positive gearing is the opposite — the property generates more income than it costs. You pay tax on the net rental income, but the property is cash-flow positive from day one. High-yield regional properties sometimes achieve this.

## Risks of Negative Gearing

- Relies on capital growth to be worthwhile — not guaranteed
- Interest rate rises increase holding costs significantly
- Vacancy periods or large repair bills can worsen losses
- Changes to tax policy could reduce or eliminate the benefit
- Reduces current cash flow, which may affect lifestyle or borrowing capacity

## Negative Gearing and Shares

Negative gearing is not exclusive to property. If you borrow to invest in shares and the interest expense exceeds dividend income, the net loss is also deductible against other income. This is less common but follows the same logic.
