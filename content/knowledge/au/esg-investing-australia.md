# ESG and Ethical Investing in Australia

Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) investing incorporates non-financial factors into investment decisions. Australia has a growing range of ESG products and strong regulatory frameworks supporting sustainable finance.

## What ESG Means

**Environmental (E)**: How a company manages its impact on the natural environment — carbon emissions, waste management, water usage, renewable energy, climate risk.

**Social (S)**: How a company manages relationships with employees, suppliers, customers, and communities — labour practices, workplace safety, data privacy, community impact.

**Governance (G)**: How a company is managed and controlled — board independence, executive pay, shareholder rights, transparency, anti-corruption.

## ESG Approaches

**Negative screening (exclusion)**: Excluding companies in sectors deemed harmful — fossil fuels, weapons, tobacco, gambling, alcohol.

**Positive screening (best-in-class)**: Selecting companies with the best ESG scores within each sector, even imperfect industries.

**ESG integration**: Incorporating ESG factors into fundamental financial analysis without mandating exclusions.

**Impact investing**: Targeting investments that generate specific positive social or environmental outcomes alongside financial returns.

**Shareholder engagement**: Using ownership stakes to influence company behaviour through voting and engagement.

## ESG Super Options

Many Australian super funds offer ethical or sustainable investment options:
- Australian Ethical Super: dedicated ethical fund, B Corp certified
- Future Super: divests from fossil fuels
- Aware Super (Socially Responsible option)
- Many large industry funds offer a sustainable or ethical choice within their investment menu

## ESG Ratings and Data

ESG ratings are provided by agencies including MSCI, Sustainalytics, and ISS. Rating methodologies differ significantly — a company may receive a high MSCI ESG rating but a low Sustainalytics score. Investors should understand the underlying methodology before relying on ESG scores.

## Does ESG Compromise Returns?

The evidence is mixed. Some studies show ESG strategies have matched or outperformed traditional benchmarks over certain periods, partly because ESG screening can exclude poorly governed companies prone to scandal and regulation risk. Others show periods of underperformance (e.g., when energy stocks were excluded during oil price surges). ESG investing is not primarily about outperformance — it is about aligning investments with values.

## ASIC and ESG Disclosure

ASIC has cracked down on "greenwashing" — making misleading ESG claims. Super funds and ETF managers must accurately represent the sustainability characteristics of their products. Investors should read the product disclosure statement (PDS) to understand exactly what is and is not screened.
