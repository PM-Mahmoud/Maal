# Franking Credits and Dividend Imputation

Australia's dividend imputation system is one of the most generous in the world for shareholders. It prevents the double taxation of company profits — tax paid by the company is credited back to shareholders.

## How Dividend Imputation Works

Australian companies pay corporate tax at 30% (or 25% for base-rate entities with turnover under $50M). When they pay a dividend from after-tax profits, they can attach "franking credits" (also called imputation credits) representing the tax already paid.

Shareholders include both the dividend and the franking credit in their assessable income, but receive a tax offset for the franking credit amount.

**Example:**
- Company earns $100 profit, pays $30 tax, distributes $70 as a fully franked dividend
- Shareholder receives: $70 cash + $30 franking credit
- Shareholder includes $100 in assessable income
- Tax on $100 at their marginal rate, minus $30 credit
- If marginal rate is 32.5%: tax = $32.50 − $30 = $2.50 net tax
- If marginal rate is 19%: tax = $19 − $30 = −$11 → $11 refund from ATO

## Refundable Franking Credits

Since 2000, excess franking credits are refundable — if the credits exceed your tax liability, you receive the difference as a cash refund. This particularly benefits low-income retirees and self-managed super funds in pension phase (0% tax rate), who receive the full franking credit as cash.

## Fully vs Partially Franked Dividends

- **Fully franked**: The company has paid full Australian corporate tax on the profits being distributed (franking level 100%).
- **Partially franked**: Some profits were not subject to Australian corporate tax (e.g., overseas income), so the dividend is only partially credited.
- **Unfranked**: No Australian corporate tax has been paid on these profits.

## Impact on Investment Returns

For Australian investors, franking credits significantly enhance the effective yield of ASX dividends. A 5% fully franked dividend is equivalent to approximately 7.1% pre-tax for a 30% taxpayer. High-yielding ASX stocks like the big four banks, BHP, and Telstra are popular partly for this reason.

## Franking Credits and ETFs

ETFs that hold Australian shares pass through the franking credits from underlying holdings. The level of franking depends on how much of the ETF's income comes from Australian companies paying fully franked dividends.

## International Shares and Franking

Foreign companies do not pay Australian corporate tax, so dividends from international shares have no franking credits. International ETFs (e.g., VGS, IWLD) distribute unfranked dividends.
