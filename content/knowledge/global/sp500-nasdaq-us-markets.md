# S&P 500, NASDAQ and US Markets

The United States has the world's largest and most liquid stock market, accounting for roughly half of global equity market capitalisation. For Australian investors, US market exposure is a core component of most diversified portfolios.

## S&P 500

The S&P 500 is widely considered the best single gauge of US large-cap equity performance. It tracks 500 of the largest US publicly traded companies selected by a committee based on market cap, liquidity, and other criteria. The index is market-cap weighted, meaning larger companies have a greater influence on its movements.

**Key characteristics:**
- Covers approximately 80% of available US market capitalisation
- Reconstituted quarterly — companies are added/removed as circumstances change
- Top holdings include Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Tesla, Berkshire Hathaway
- Historically returned approximately 10% per year before inflation over the long run

## NASDAQ Composite and NASDAQ 100

The NASDAQ Composite tracks all companies listed on the NASDAQ exchange — over 3,000 stocks, heavily weighted towards technology. The NASDAQ 100 (tracked by QQQ in the US, NDQ in Australia) covers the 100 largest non-financial NASDAQ companies. Both indices have significant overlap with the S&P 500 since major tech companies appear in both.

The NASDAQ 100 is more volatile than the S&P 500 due to its concentration in high-growth technology companies. It dramatically outperformed during the 2010s tech boom but also suffered deeper drawdowns during the 2000–2002 dot-com crash and 2022 rate-rise environment.

## Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA)

The Dow is the oldest US index, tracking 30 large US companies selected by the editors of the Wall Street Journal. It is price-weighted (higher-priced shares have more influence), making it less representative than the S&P 500. The Dow is widely reported in media but less useful as an investment benchmark.

## US Market Sectors

The S&P 500 is divided into 11 sectors:
1. Information Technology (~30%): Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA
2. Healthcare (~13%): UnitedHealth, Johnson & Johnson, Eli Lilly
3. Financials (~13%): JPMorgan, Berkshire, Visa
4. Consumer Discretionary (~10%): Amazon, Tesla, McDonald's
5. Communication Services (~9%): Alphabet, Meta, Netflix
6. Industrials (~9%): Boeing, GE, Caterpillar
7. Consumer Staples (~6%): Procter & Gamble, Coca-Cola
8. Energy (~4%): ExxonMobil, Chevron
9. Real Estate (~2%), Utilities (~2%), Materials (~2%)

## How to Invest in US Markets from Australia

**Via ASX-listed ETFs (simplest)**:
- IVV (iShares S&P 500 ETF): tracks S&P 500, traded in AUD on ASX
- NDQ (Betashares NASDAQ 100): tracks NASDAQ 100, traded in AUD
- SPY, QQQ (US-listed): require a US brokerage account

**Via international brokers** (Stake, Interactive Brokers, CommSec International):
- Buy US-listed shares and ETFs directly in USD
- Need to complete W-8BEN form to reduce US withholding tax from 30% to 15%
- Currency conversion costs apply

## Currency Risk

When investing in US markets from Australia, you bear USD/AUD currency risk. If the AUD strengthens against the USD, your US investment returns in AUD terms are reduced. Conversely, AUD weakness (common during global downturns) boosts US investment returns in AUD. Many Australian investors choose unhedged exposure as natural currency diversification.
