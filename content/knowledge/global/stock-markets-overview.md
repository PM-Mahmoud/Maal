# Global Stock Markets Overview

Stock markets are organised exchanges where buyers and sellers trade shares in publicly listed companies. Major global exchanges are interconnected — events in one market quickly affect others through investor sentiment, currency flows, and capital movements.

## Major Stock Exchanges

**New York Stock Exchange (NYSE)**: The world's largest stock exchange by market capitalisation, located on Wall Street, New York. Home to iconic companies including Berkshire Hathaway, JPMorgan Chase, Exxon, and most major US industrial and financial companies. Listed companies have market caps totalling over $25 trillion. Open Monday–Friday 9:30am–4:00pm ET.

**NASDAQ**: America's second-largest exchange and home to most of the world's major technology companies — Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Amazon, Alphabet (Google), Meta, and Tesla. NASDAQ is an electronic exchange with no physical trading floor. It is where growth-oriented and technology companies typically list.

**London Stock Exchange (LSE)**: Europe's largest exchange, home to FTSE 100 companies including HSBC, Shell, AstraZeneca, Unilever, and BP. The UK's market capitalisation exceeds £3 trillion. The FTSE 100 index tracks the 100 largest companies by market cap.

**Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE/JPX)**: Asia's largest exchange, home to Toyota, Sony, SoftBank, Nintendo, and Honda. The Nikkei 225 and TOPIX are the primary indices. Japan is the third-largest stock market globally.

**Shanghai Stock Exchange (SSE) and Shenzhen Stock Exchange (SZSE)**: China's two major exchanges, with a combined market cap making China the second-largest stock market globally. Foreign investor access is restricted but improving through Stock Connect programs.

**Hong Kong Stock Exchange (HKEX)**: A key gateway for international investment in China, hosting both Hong Kong companies and Chinese companies via H-shares. The Hang Seng Index tracks the top 50 companies.

**Euronext**: A pan-European exchange operating in Amsterdam, Brussels, Dublin, Lisbon, Milan, Oslo, and Paris. Home to LVMH, ASML, Airbus, and many major European companies.

**Deutsche Börse (Frankfurt)**: Germany's primary exchange. The DAX index tracks 40 major German companies including Volkswagen, Siemens, SAP, and Allianz.

**Australian Securities Exchange (ASX)**: Australia's primary exchange, ranking among the top 15 globally. Dominated by financial services (banks) and resources (mining) companies.

**Toronto Stock Exchange (TSX)**: Canada's primary exchange, strong in energy, mining, and financials. Home to the TSX Composite index.

## Market Hours and Overlap

Different exchanges operate in different time zones, creating 24-hour global trading. Key overlap periods:
- **European/US overlap (2pm–4pm London)**: Highest volume period globally
- **Asian/European open (8am–9am London)**: Moderate activity
- **For Australians**: US markets close around 6am–8am AEST, Sydney opens at 10am

## Market Capitalisation by Region

- United States: ~50% of global market cap
- Europe: ~15%
- Asia Pacific (ex-China): ~15%
- China: ~10%
- Rest of world: ~10%

## How Australian Investors Access Global Markets

1. **ASX-listed international ETFs**: VGS, IVV, NDQ — track global indices, traded in AUD
2. **Directly via international brokers**: Stake, Interactive Brokers — buy US/global stocks in USD
3. **ADRs**: Some foreign companies trade as American Depositary Receipts on US exchanges
4. **Managed funds**: International managed funds available through platforms like Netwealth and HUB24
