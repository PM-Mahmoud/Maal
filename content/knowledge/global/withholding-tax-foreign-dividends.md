# Withholding Tax on Foreign Dividends

When Australian investors receive dividends from international shares or ETFs, the company's home country may withhold a portion of the dividend as a tax before it reaches you. Understanding this prevents surprises on your tax return.

## How Withholding Tax Works

Say you hold US shares and receive a US$100 dividend. The US applies a 30% withholding tax by default, so only US$70 reaches your account. You then include the gross dividend (US$100) in your Australian tax return, but claim the US$30 withheld as a Foreign Income Tax Offset (FITO), reducing your Australian tax liability.

## The Australia-US Tax Treaty and W-8BEN

Australia has a tax treaty with the US that reduces dividend withholding from 30% to 15% for eligible Australian investors. To access the reduced rate, you must complete and submit a **W-8BEN form** to your broker. Most Australian international brokers (Stake, Interactive Brokers, CommSec International) collect this during account setup or periodically.

Without a W-8BEN, you pay the full 30% withholding rate, which you can still claim as a FITO, but you must wait until you lodge your Australian tax return to receive any credit.

## ASX-Listed ETFs vs Direct International Shares

When you hold an ASX-listed international ETF (like VGS or IVV), the ETF itself handles withholding tax. The fund receives dividends net of foreign withholding tax, and the Australian managed fund tax laws determine how much you can claim as FITO. In practice, investors in ASX-listed international ETFs often cannot fully claim all foreign withholding tax paid at the fund level — this is a known inefficiency.

Direct international share ownership allows full FITO claims, but requires more tax record-keeping.

## Foreign Income Tax Offset (FITO)

The FITO is the mechanism by which Australian investors avoid double taxation. You:
1. Include the gross foreign dividend in your assessable income
2. Calculate Australian tax on that income
3. Reduce the tax by the lesser of (a) foreign tax paid or (b) the Australian tax on the foreign income

If foreign withholding tax exceeds your Australian tax liability on that income, the excess cannot be refunded — it is lost. This particularly affects low-income earners.

## Record Keeping

For direct international investing, maintain records of:
- Foreign income received (in AUD at the exchange rate on the date of receipt)
- Foreign tax withheld
- Currency conversion rates
- Purchase and sale prices in original currency and AUD equivalent

Your international broker's annual tax statement usually provides this information in a format usable for your Australian tax return.
