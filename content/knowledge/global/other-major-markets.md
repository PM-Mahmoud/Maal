# European, Asian and Emerging Markets

A globally diversified portfolio includes exposure beyond the US. European, Asian, and emerging markets offer different growth drivers, valuations, and risk profiles.

## European Markets

**FTSE 100 (UK)**: Tracks 100 largest companies on the London Stock Exchange. Heavily weighted towards global companies in financials (HSBC, Barclays), energy (Shell, BP), healthcare (AstraZeneca, GSK), and consumer goods (Unilever, Diageo). Despite being a "UK" index, most FTSE 100 companies derive the majority of revenue internationally. The FTSE 250 (mid-caps) is more representative of the UK domestic economy.

**DAX (Germany)**: Germany's benchmark index, tracking 40 companies on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange. Heavily weighted towards industrials, automotive (Volkswagen, BMW, Mercedes), chemicals (BASF), software (SAP), insurance (Allianz, Munich Re), and healthcare (Bayer). Germany is Europe's largest economy and a major global exporter.

**CAC 40 (France)**: France's 40 largest companies. Includes luxury goods (LVMH, Kering, Hermès), energy (TotalEnergies), aerospace (Airbus), banking (BNP Paribas), and retail (Carrefour).

**Euro Stoxx 50**: Tracks 50 blue-chip companies from the Eurozone. Provides broad European exposure including German, French, Dutch, Spanish, and Italian companies. Tracked by EUO and similar ETFs.

## Asian Markets

**Nikkei 225 (Japan)**: Japan's most quoted index, tracking 225 companies on the Tokyo Stock Exchange. Price-weighted like the Dow. Major companies include Toyota, Sony, SoftBank, Nintendo, Honda, Canon, and Mitsubishi. Japan is undergoing significant changes including corporate governance reform and inflation returning after decades of deflation.

**TOPIX (Japan)**: Broader and more representative than the Nikkei, tracking all First Section companies on the TSE. Better gauge of overall Japanese equity performance.

**Hang Seng (Hong Kong)**: Tracks 50 largest companies listed in Hong Kong, including major Chinese companies (Alibaba, Tencent, Meituan) and local conglomerates (CK Hutchison, Link REIT). Sentiment closely tied to China's economic outlook and regulatory environment.

**Kospi (South Korea)**: South Korea's major index, home to Samsung Electronics, Hyundai, LG, and SK Hynix — global leaders in semiconductors, electronics, and automotive.

**Sensex / Nifty 50 (India)**: India's two main indices. India has become one of the world's fastest-growing major economies, attracting significant global investment. Major sectors include IT services (Infosys, TCS, Wipro), banking (HDFC, ICICI), and consumer goods.

## Emerging Markets

Emerging markets (EM) are economies transitioning from developing to developed status. Major EM economies include China, India, Brazil, South Korea, Taiwan, Saudi Arabia, and South Africa.

**Characteristics:**
- Higher growth potential but higher volatility
- Currency risk is more significant
- Political and regulatory risk is higher
- Markets may be less liquid

**Key EM indices:**
- MSCI Emerging Markets Index: the primary benchmark
- MSCI Emerging Markets IMI: includes small-caps

**EM ETFs available to AU investors:**
- VGE (Vanguard Emerging Markets ETF on ASX)
- IEM (iShares MSCI EM ETF on ASX)

## Frontier Markets

Frontier markets are less developed than emerging markets — countries like Vietnam, Bangladesh, Kenya, Morocco, and Romania. Very high risk but potential for high growth. Accessible primarily through specialist ETFs or managed funds.

## Why Global Diversification Matters

Different economies are at different stages of the business cycle at any given time. When US equities are overvalued or experiencing a downturn, European or Asian markets may be performing differently. Global diversification reduces concentration risk and provides access to sectors and companies not available on the ASX.
