# How Australians Invest in Global Markets

Australian investors have excellent access to global markets through multiple channels. Whether you want to own Apple shares directly, invest in a US index fund, or gain exposure to European or Asian equities, here are your options.

## Option 1: ASX-Listed International ETFs (Simplest)

The easiest way to invest globally without leaving the ASX. You use your existing Australian broker account, trade in AUD, and the ETF handles currency conversion internally.

**Pros**: Simple, low cost, tax-effective (no foreign tax complications), CHESS sponsored
**Cons**: Limited to available ETF products, no individual stock selection

Key products:
- VGS / VGAD: global developed markets
- IVV: S&P 500
- NDQ: NASDAQ 100
- VGE: emerging markets
- IWLD: global all-country

## Option 2: Direct US/International Shares via Brokers

For buying individual companies (Apple, Tesla, Amazon) or US-listed ETFs (SPY, QQQ, VTI):

**Stake**: Commission-free US share trading for Australians. User-friendly app.
**Interactive Brokers (IBKR)**: Professional-grade platform, very low fees, access to 150+ markets globally. Suited to more active investors.
**CommSec International**: ASX's parent company's international platform. Higher fees but familiar brand.
**Pearler**: Focuses on long-term investing, supports US and Australian shares.

**W-8BEN Form**: Required for Australian investors trading US securities. This treaty form reduces US withholding tax on dividends from 30% to 15% (the Australia-US tax treaty rate). Most brokers collect this automatically.

## Option 3: Managed Funds with International Exposure

Investment platforms like Netwealth, HUB24, BT Panorama, and Macquarie Wrap offer access to hundreds of managed funds with international exposure. These often require minimum investments and are typically accessed through a financial adviser.

## Option 4: Listed Investment Companies (LICs) and Listed Investment Trusts (LITs)

LICs are ASX-listed companies that invest in a portfolio of assets. Several LICs focus on international shares:
- Magellan Financial Group products
- Platinum Asset Management: focuses on Asian and global value investing
- MFF Capital: concentrated global equity portfolio

## Tax Implications of International Investing

**Dividends**: Subject to Australian income tax. Foreign withholding taxes (typically 15% via treaty) are a foreign income tax offset (FITO) reducing your Australian tax liability.

**Capital gains**: CGT applies when you sell. The 50% discount applies to assets held over 12 months, even if held on an international exchange.

**Currency gains/losses**: Converting between AUD and foreign currencies is a CGT event if done for investment purposes (not personal travel). In practice, currency gains/losses are bundled into your overall capital gain on the investment.

**Record keeping**: You must report all foreign income to the ATO, even if tax was withheld overseas. Your international broker will provide annual tax statements.

## CHESS vs Custodian for International Shares

International shares held directly (not through ASX-listed ETFs) are typically held in a custodian structure — the broker holds the shares in their name on your behalf. This is standard practice for international investing and carries minimal practical risk with regulated brokers, but differs from the CHESS sponsorship model used for ASX shares.
