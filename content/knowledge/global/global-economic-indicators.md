# Key Global Economic Indicators

Economic indicators are statistics that provide insight into the health and direction of an economy. Understanding these indicators helps investors interpret market movements and anticipate central bank policy.

## Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

GDP measures the total value of goods and services produced in a country. It is the broadest measure of economic activity.

- **Real GDP**: Adjusted for inflation — the most meaningful measure
- **GDP growth rate**: Quarter-on-quarter or year-on-year change. Two consecutive quarters of negative growth = recession (technical definition)
- **US GDP**: The world's largest economy; US growth significantly influences global markets
- **Global GDP**: World Bank and IMF publish global GDP estimates. Emerging markets (China, India) are growing faster than developed economies

## Consumer Price Index (CPI) and Inflation

CPI tracks changes in the price of a representative basket of goods and services. It is the primary measure of inflation.

- Australia's CPI is published quarterly by the ABS
- The RBA targets 2%–3% annual CPI growth
- The US CPI is published monthly by the Bureau of Labor Statistics; the Fed targets 2%
- **Core inflation**: Excludes volatile food and energy prices — watched closely by central banks
- **PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditures)**: The Fed's preferred inflation measure (in addition to CPI)

## Unemployment Rate

The percentage of the labour force that is unemployed and actively seeking work.

- Australia's full employment is considered approximately 4%–4.5% (NAIRU)
- Low unemployment typically precedes inflation (more workers with income → more spending)
- The US non-farm payrolls report (released first Friday of each month) is one of the most market-moving economic releases globally

## Interest Rates and Central Banks

The interest rate decisions of major central banks move global markets:

- **RBA** (Australia): Sets the cash rate, meets 11 times per year
- **Federal Reserve** (US): Sets the federal funds rate, meets 8 times per year (FOMC meetings)
- **European Central Bank** (ECB): Sets rates for the Eurozone, meets every 6 weeks
- **Bank of Japan** (BOJ): Historically ultra-accommodative, but shifting in 2024–25
- **Bank of England** (BOE): Sets UK base rate

When the Fed raises rates, global capital flows toward USD assets (higher yield), often causing other currencies including AUD to weaken.

## Purchasing Managers Index (PMI)

PMI surveys measure business conditions in manufacturing (Manufacturing PMI) and services (Services PMI). A reading above 50 indicates expansion; below 50 indicates contraction. PMI data is released monthly and is a leading indicator — companies adjust production plans ahead of actual economic changes.

## Trade Balance

The difference between a country's exports and imports. Australia has a persistent trade surplus driven by commodity exports (iron ore, coal, LNG, gold). Trade data affects the AUD and economic growth forecasts.

## Consumer Confidence and Retail Sales

Consumer confidence surveys (Australia: Westpac-Melbourne Institute Consumer Sentiment; US: Conference Board Consumer Confidence) measure household optimism. Retail sales data shows actual consumer spending. Together they indicate whether households are likely to spend or save.

## Why These Matter for Your Investments

- High inflation → central banks raise rates → bond prices fall, growth stocks underperform
- Strong employment + growth → corporate earnings typically strong → shares tend to do well
- Rate cuts expected → bond prices rise, rate-sensitive sectors (property, utilities, REITs) often benefit
- Global slowdown → commodity prices fall → AUD weakens, Australian resources companies underperform
